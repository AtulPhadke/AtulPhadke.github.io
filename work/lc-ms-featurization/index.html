During my ML engineering co-op at Matterworks.ai, I built a C++/Python algorithm that links MS2 spectra to the MS1 peak-shaped features they came from. It works on XICs (extracted ion chromatograms).

Significantly improved

lipid analyte detection over the industry tools MZmine and MS-DIAL

Proprietary work with no public code, so the figures here use simulated data and don’t show the method.

Finding features

  1. One molecule, one m/z

    A molecule comes off the column over a few seconds, so its signal at one m/z rises and falls as a peak.

  2. A feature

    The peak has fainter isotope copies about 1 Da heavier. Boxed together, they make one feature.

  3. MZmine’s pass

    Over about 11 minutes this m/z holds 11 features. MZmine calls the peaks that clear the noise and finds 5.

  4. Our pass

    Ours finds all 11, including the 6 MZmine missed: four near the noise, a shoulder on a bigger peak and a co-eluting neighbour.

Linking MS2 to MS1

MS1 gives a molecule’s mass, but many molecules share one. An MS2 scan breaks one precursor into fragments, and that pattern identifies it.

It only helps once the spectrum is tied to the feature its precursor came from. That linking is what my algorithm does.
